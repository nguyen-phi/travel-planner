# Airline Alliances

## What an alliance is

An airline alliance is a group of airlines that cooperate. Miles and status earned with one
member work across all of them: earn miles in one program when flying any member, redeem
miles from one program to book any member's flights, and use elite perks (lounges, priority
boarding, free bags) across the whole group. This is why the strategy is one program per
alliance, not one per airline.

## The three global alliances

Star Alliance — main US airline is United. Also Lufthansa, Air Canada, ANA, Singapore,
Swiss, Turkish, Copa, Avianca, EVA, Austrian, Brussels.

Oneworld — main US airline is American. Also British Airways, Iberia, Qatar, Cathay Pacific,
Japan Airlines, Qantas, Finnair, Malaysia, Royal Jordanian. Alaska is a member.

SkyTeam — main US airline is Delta. Also Air France, KLM, Korean Air, Aeromexico, and Virgin
Atlantic as a partner (not full member).

No alliance: Southwest, JetBlue, Emirates, Etihad, Hawaiian (own partnerships instead).

## Picking a primary program by home airport

Anchor on the alliance whose main airline has the most nonstops from the user's home airport.

Examples:
- SFO (San Francisco): United hub → Star Alliance / United MileagePlus as primary. Alaska
  (Oneworld) strong as secondary, especially for West Coast and SJC. Delta a distant third.
- SJC (San Jose): dominated by Southwest and Alaska, with some United. Alaska (Oneworld) or
  United (Star) as primary; Southwest for cheap cash fares.
- ATL, DTW, MSP, SLC: Delta hubs → SkyTeam / Delta.
- DFW, CLT, PHX, MIA, ORD: American hubs → Oneworld / American.
- ORD, DEN, IAH, EWR, IAD: United hubs → Star Alliance / United.

General rule: whichever carrier flies the most nonstops from home anchors earning and the
status chase. Add one secondary program in a second alliance if the user regularly flies a
different carrier.
