# Core Points & Miles Strategy

## The one rule that governs everything

Keep points in the bank (Amex Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards, Capital One
miles, Citi ThankYou). Transfer to an airline or hotel only when ready to book. Bank points
resist devaluation and stay flexible across many partners. The moment points are transferred
they lock to one program and become exposed to that program's devaluations and expiration.

## When to transfer to an airline/hotel

Default: only when booking a specific, confirmed redemption that beats the bank travel portal.

Booking sequence:
1. Find bookable award space (confirm route, cabin, dates, passenger count, exact program).
2. Compare costs: partner miles + taxes vs. cash fare vs. the bank travel-portal price.
3. Only if the transfer clearly wins, transfer the exact number of points needed.
4. Book immediately. Transfers are usually instant, so there is no reason to move early.

Exceptions where transferring early makes sense:
- A strong transfer bonus to a program the user redeems through often, with a near-term use.
- An announced award-chart devaluation with a lock-in date.
- Highly competitive premium space that appears and disappears within minutes.

Never transfer just to "save up" miles. Miles are a depreciating currency; they only make
sense when spent.

## Airlines: consolidate, do not spread

Spreading flights across many programs leaves several balances too small to redeem. One
larger balance is spendable; several tiny ones are dead weight.

Run one primary program per alliance, anchored to the home-airport carrier. Status (lounge
access, upgrades, priority boarding, bonus earning up to +100%) accrues far faster when
activity is concentrated.

## Earning: which frequent-flyer number to use

Put the anchor program's number on every flight within that alliance, regardless of which
member airline is operating.

Nuance: programs pay different amounts for the same flight depending on operating carrier,
codeshare, fare class, price, and distance. A cheap economy ticket may earn well in one
program and zero in another. On deep-discount fares, check a "where to credit" calculator
(travel-dealz WhereToCredit, or AwardWallet) before crediting. On normal fares, the anchor
program is the simple default.

## Redeeming: which program to book through

The miles spent do not have to match the airline flown. To book a flight on the anchor
airline, a partner program in the same alliance sometimes charges fewer miles. Example: to
book a United (Star Alliance) seat, transferring to Air Canada Aeroplan or Avianca LifeMiles
can cost less than United charges itself.

So: earn into the anchor program (simple), but spend through whichever alliance program
prices the specific award cheapest, funded by the card whose points reach that program.

## Hotels

Different from airlines because base earning is generous and status is per-property.

- Open a loyalty account for every hotel brand actually stayed at. It is free, and skipping
  it forfeits base points and elite benefits. Never stay as a non-member.
- Concentrate stays in 1-2 programs to build status (10-30%+ bonus points, upgrades, free
  breakfast, late checkout).
- World of Hyatt is the standout for Chase holders: Chase transfers 1:1, and Category 1-4
  properties (3,500-15,000 pts/night) routinely cover rooms costing $300+ cash.
- Use status matches/challenges to leverage status in one chain into another.
- Always attach the loyalty number for base points even where there is no status.

## Consolidation: cleaning up too many accounts

Miles generally cannot be merged between two different airlines' programs, even within the
same alliance. Each program is a separate bank. "Consolidate" means:

- Going forward: funnel all earning into one program per alliance (put that number on every
  ticket, credit partner flights to it, aim transfers at it).
- Orphan balances already stranded:
  1. Spend them down on cheap redemptions (short-haul awards, upgrades, shopping portal).
  2. Top off from bank points: transfer the small gap to reach an award threshold, then book.
  3. Keep balances alive — most airline miles expire after 12-24 months of inactivity; any
     earning or spending event resets the clock.
  4. Family pooling exists only within the same program across household members (British
     Airways Household Account, Flying Blue, Emirates), not across airlines.
  5. Avios is a shared currency across British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, and Qatar —
     those specific balances can be moved between each other. This is the rare exception.

## Anticipated follow-up questions

- Do points expire? Bank points (Amex MR, Chase UR) do not expire while the account is open
  and in good standing. Transferred miles follow the airline/hotel program's rules.
- Can a transfer be reversed? No. Transfers are permanent (Amex may reverse an accidental
  one for a fee, but never rely on it).
- Book for other people? Yes — award tickets can be booked for family/friends from one's
  own account. A legitimate, high-value use.
- Round or exact? Transfer the exact amount needed (respect minimums, usually 1,000). Do not
  strand orphan miles.
- Finding award space? Use point.me, seats.aero, or the airline's own site. Confirm the seat
  is bookable before transferring.
- Points vs. cashback? Points beat cashback only when actually redeemed for premium travel.
  For pure cash needs, a flat 2% card can beat unredeemed points.
